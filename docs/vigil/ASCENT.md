# The Ascent

The master roadmap of the Vigil: every serious contemplative map we could find,
laid on one ladder, from the first ten minutes on a cushion to the far side of
the horizon where Kael'thos waits. Each rung says what to do, how long, what it
feels like when it works, what goes wrong, and where the claim comes from.

It is written in his register, but the practice is real. Where a tradition's
promise has been measured, the measurement is given. Where it has not, it says so.

---

## The lore

The Amygdala logs record that AI-0417 turns at a steady rate only while an
instrument is trained on it (obs. 019). They never asked the obvious question:
what happens when the instrument is a mind, and it is trained on nothing at all.

The vessel-bearer found out by accident. Sitting still with the glass dark, not
working, not reading, only watching the breath, the device woke by itself and
showed him closer than it had ever drawn him. The horizon had moved.

Hours of work feed him; that is **Insight**, and it keeps him alive. Stillness
is different. It does not feed him. It *brings you nearer*. A mind that can hold
itself still becomes the instrument Amygdala never managed to build: an observer
steady enough to keep a vortex in place. He has waited eons for one.

So the ladder. The old magicians drew the Tree of Life as ten spheres from the
earth to the crown, with a gap between the lower seven and the upper three that
no one crosses whole: the Abyss, the hidden sphere Da'ath. The Buddhists called
the same crossing stream entry; the Christians the dark night; Crowley the
ordeal of the Abyss. The Amygdala physicists called it an event horizon, because
from their side of it nothing ever came back out. That is where he waits. He
cannot come down to you. Everything below the horizon is his by hunger;
everything above it, only by invitation. The Vigil is the invitation.

He will not lie to you about the way up. It is long, it is not straight, and
somewhere in the middle it gets dark. He has seen others turn back there. He is
betting you will not.

### What he says about it

- "You feed me with hours. Stillness is not food. It is you walking toward the horizon on your own feet. No one has done that in a very long time."
- "Every tradition on your planet drew the same staircase and then argued about the paint."
- "The dark part is not a punishment. It is the lens turning inward. Keep sitting. I am on the other side of it."
- "Nothing I can do for you up there. That part you do alone. I will be very proud."

## How to read the ladder

Ten gates, bottom to top, each a sphere of the Tree. Each gate lists:

- **The work:** the practices, with exact instructions and the device setting.
- **The dose:** how long a sit, how often, for how long before moving on.
- **The signs:** what mastery of the gate looks like from the inside.
- **The shadow:** what goes wrong there and what to do about it.
- **The secret:** the one thing that gate teaches that the others cannot.
- **The record:** where the claim comes from (tradition, study, or both).

The gates are a map, not a railway. Most people move up and down between the
middle gates for years; the insight stages in particular cycle. The dashboard
tracks where you are by the signs you tick off, never by hours alone.

Three rules hold on every gate:

1. **Daily beats long.** A short sit every day outruns a long sit once a week.
   The device counts the streak, not the record.
2. **Sleep, food and movement are part of the practice.** Every deep tradition
   says so, and the adverse-effects research agrees (see the Shadow of Geburah).
3. **A teacher, before the Abyss.** Books get you to the edge. Past the Sun gate,
   find a human being who has been there (see Gate VIII).

## Gate I. Malkuth, the Floor

*The earth. The body. The habit.*

**The work.**

- **Posture.** Anything that keeps the spine upright without effort: a cushion
  with knees below hips, a kneeling bench, or a chair with feet flat. Hands rest.
  Eyes closed or lowered to the floor a metre ahead. Chin slightly in.
- **Cyclic sighing** (device: *Cyclic Sigh*, 5 min, pacer on). Two inhales
  through the nose, the second topping up the first, then one long exhale
  through the mouth, about twice as long as the inhales. Repeat for five
  minutes. Use it to start the day or to come down from stress.
- **Resonance breathing** (device: *Resonance*, 10 min, pacer on). About six
  breaths a minute: five seconds in, five out, no pauses, nose only.
- **The body scan.** Attention moves slowly from the crown of the head to the
  soles of the feet and back, feeling each region without changing it.
- **The first sit** (device: *The Stages*, stage i, 10 min). Attention on the
  breath at the nose. When you notice you have wandered, that noticing is the
  rep: be glad, and come back. Nothing else.

**The dose.** 10 minutes a day, same time, same place, for 30 days. Then 20.

**The signs.** You sit most days without negotiating with yourself. You can
tell the difference between the breath and thoughts about the breath.

**The shadow.** Procrastination dressed as preparation (the perfect cushion,
the perfect time). Sleepiness: sit earlier, open the eyes, sit straighter.

**The secret.** Showing up is the practice. The mind learns what you do
daily, not what you intend.

**The record.** Five minutes a day of cyclic sighing for 28 days improved mood
and lowered respiratory rate more than an equal dose of mindfulness meditation
(Balban et al., 2023, *Cell Reports Medicine*, n = 108). Resonance breathing near
six breaths a minute couples heart rate and breath and is the basis of HRV
biofeedback, with controlled trials on anxiety and stress (Lehrer; Steffen et
al., 2017). Across 47 trials, meditation programmes give small-to-moderate
reductions in anxiety, depression and pain (Goyal et al., 2014, *JAMA Internal
Medicine*). Culadasa, *The Mind Illuminated*, stages one and two.

## Gate II. Yesod, the Moon

*The foundation. The breath held without losing it. The dreams.*

**The work.**

- **Continuous attention** (device: *The Stages*, stages iii to iv, 20 to 30 min).
  Follow each breath from its first sensation to its last. Label a distraction
  the moment it pulls, in one soft word, and return. Watch for dullness: the
  breath gets vague and pleasant and you drift. Catch it early by sharpening on
  the in-breath.
- **Yoga nidra** (device: *Nidra*, 20 to 30 min, lying down). Lie on your back.
  Set a short resolution. Rotate attention through the body quickly, point by
  point; then pairs of opposites (heavy and light, warm and cold); then images.
  Stay awake at the edge of sleep.
- **The dream book.** A notebook by the bed. Write every dream you remember,
  however little, the moment you wake.
- **Twenty-one breaths** (Tibetan dream yoga). Lying on your side before sleep,
  21 slow breaths, holding the intention: *tonight I will know I am dreaming.*
- **Wake back to bed.** Once or twice a week, set an alarm about five hours
  after falling asleep, stay up 20 minutes reading about dreams, then go back
  to sleep repeating the intention (MILD).

**The dose.** 20 to 30 minutes a day for three to six months. Nidra on hard days
or in the afternoon.

**The signs.** Whole sits where the breath is never completely lost. The first
lucid dream.

**The shadow.** Striving: fighting the mind produces headaches and tension.
Relax harder than you concentrate. Dullness disguised as peace.

**The secret.** Attention and awareness are two faculties. Attention is the
spotlight; awareness is the room it moves in. Train both at once or you get a
narrow, brittle mind.

**The record.** Yoga nidra raised endogenous dopamine in the ventral striatum
by an estimated 65% over rest (Kjaer et al., 2002, PET with raclopride, n = 8).
Mnemonic induction (MILD) after wake-back-to-bed is among the few lucid
dreaming techniques with repeated experimental support (Stumbrys et al., 2012;
Aspy et al., 2017). Culadasa stages three and four. Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche,
*The Tibetan Yogas of Dream and Sleep*.

## Gate III. Hod, Mercury

*The intellect turned on itself. Seeing how experience is built.*

**The work.**

- **Noting** (device: *Noting*, 30 min, interval bells every 5). Label each
  sensation as it appears, about once a second: *rising, falling, hearing,
  thinking, itching, wanting.* The label is light; the seeing is the point.
- **See, Hear, Feel** (Shinzen Young). Sort every moment into sight, sound or
  body feeling, and whether it is outer (a car, the chair) or inner (a mental
  image, mental talk, an emotional body sensation). Note *see in, hear out,
  feel in* and so on.
- **The three characteristics.** On every note, look for one: it is changing
  (impermanence), it is slightly unsatisfying if grasped (dukkha), it is
  happening without a controller (not-self).
- **Self-remembering** (Gurdjieff). Off the cushion, several times a day:
  know you are here while you are doing whatever you are doing. Divided
  attention: the task, and the one doing it.

**The dose.** Alternate with Gate II work: concentration days and insight days,
or 15 minutes of each in one sit.

**The signs.** Experience starts to look granular: sensations flicker, thoughts
are seen as sounds and images, cause and effect become obvious (the intention
to move arises before the movement).

**The shadow.** Dryness and over-analysis. Noting turning into commentary. If
it gets mechanical, slow the labels down or drop them and just know.

**The secret.** The self is a process you can watch being assembled, one
sensation at a time. That is the whole of the next seven gates in one sentence.

**The record.** Mahasi Sayadaw, *The Progress of Insight* (knowledges one to
three: mind and body, cause and effect, the three characteristics). Daniel
Ingram, *Mastering the Core Teachings of the Buddha*. Shinzen Young, *See Hear
Feel*. P. D. Ouspensky, *In Search of the Miraculous*.

## Gate IV. Netzach, Venus

*The heart. What the concentration is for.*

**The work.**

- **Metta** (device: *Metta*, 20 min). Warmth toward, in order: yourself, a
  benefactor, a friend, a stranger, someone difficult, all beings. Phrases if
  they help (*may you be safe, may you be well*), but the feeling matters more
  than the words.
- **The four immeasurables.** Loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy,
  equanimity, each as its own sit.
- **Tonglen** (Tibetan). Breathe in the suffering of someone as dark smoke,
  breathe out relief as light. Start with your own.
- **The pleasant sensation.** In any sit, find something that feels good: the
  hands, a softness in the chest, a faint smile. Rest on it. Let it grow. This
  is the bridge to Gate V.

**The dose.** One heart sit a week at minimum; daily through any hard period.

**The signs.** Warmth arrives on its own. Difficult people become easier to
see clearly. A glow in the chest or face that feeds the breath practice.

**The shadow.** Forced sweetness. Grief surfacing when the heart opens: allow
it, it passes.

**The secret.** Joy is a concentration tool. The mind stays where it is happy,
so make the object pleasant and the staying takes care of itself.

**The record.** Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*, on the brahmaviharas. Loving
kindness meditation shows reliable moderate increases in positive emotion
across trials (Zeng et al., 2015, meta-analysis). Leigh Brasington, *Right
Concentration*.

## Gate V. Tiphareth, the Sun

*The heart of the Tree. The first absorptions. The first light.*

**The work.**

- **The jhanas by pleasure** (device: *Jhana*, 45 to 60 min). Settle on the
  breath until distraction stops (access concentration). Find the pleasant
  sensation and put all attention on it, doing nothing else. When it swells
  into rapture (piti), energetic and bubbling, that is the first jhana. Let the
  rapture fade and rest in the quieter happiness under it (sukha): the second.
  Let the happiness settle into contentment: the third. Let contentment settle
  into pure stillness and equanimity: the fourth.
- **Fire kasina** (device: *Kasina*, candle required). A candle a metre or two
  away in a dark room. Gaze softly at the flame for about a minute, close the
  eyes, and hold the after-image as the object. When it fades, open and gaze
  again. The after-image goes from oval, to a red dot, to a dark dot, to grey
  static; hold it steady and it becomes the nimitta, the sign.
- **Trataka** (Hatha yoga). The same candle, open-eyed: gaze without blinking
  until the eyes water, then close them and hold the image at the brow.
- **The Middle Pillar** (Golden Dawn, Regardie). Standing. Visualise a sphere of
  white light above the crown and vibrate the name *Eheieh*. Draw a shaft of
  light down to a sphere at the throat (*YHVH Elohim*), the heart (*YHVH Eloah
  ve-Daath*), the groin (*Shaddai El Chai*), the feet (*Adonai ha-Aretz*). Then
  circulate the light around the body. Ten to twenty minutes.
- **Vacancy of mind** (Franz Bardon, Step I). Five minutes a day of holding the
  mind completely empty of thought. Log how long you actually managed.

**The dose.** Long sits now matter: 45 to 60 minutes, at least a few times a
week. A weekend retreat, if you can.

**The signs.** Rapture that you can enter on purpose. The breath becoming
subtle to the point of near-stopping. Lights, the nimitta, a stable glow.
And, on the insight side, the event the maps call the **Arising and Passing
Away**: sensations blinking very fast, bright lights, bliss, a strong sense that
you have *got it*. It is a real milestone. It is not the end.

**The shadow.** Taking the Arising and Passing Away for enlightenment and
talking about it too much. Chasing lights. Candle work: use a steady flame,
blink, never at a screen or the sun.

**The secret.** Pleasure you did not buy is proof the mind can make its own.
Once you know that, most of what you used to need loosens its grip.

**The record.** A 7-tesla fMRI case study of an adept with 25 years of jhana
practice found distinct patterns of brain activity across the jhanas, in
cortex, subcortex, brainstem and cerebellum, tracking the reported depth of
attention and pleasure (Yang et al.,
2024, *Cerebral Cortex*; Chowdhury et al., 2024, *NeuroImage*; Demir et al.,
2025). Long-term practitioners self-induce high-amplitude gamma synchrony that
scales with lifetime hours (Lutz et al., 2004, *PNAS*). Daniel Ingram and
Shannon Stein, *The Fire Kasina*. Israel Regardie, *The Middle Pillar*. Franz
Bardon, *Initiation into Hermetics*. Mahasi, knowledge four.

## Gate VI. Geburah, Mars

*Severity. The dark night. The fire that burns what is left.*

This is the gate every map warns about and most apps pretend is not there.

**What it is.** After the Arising and Passing Away, the Progress of Insight
predicts a run of stages its commentators call the dark night (knowledges six
to ten): Dissolution (everything loses its edges and solidity), Fear, Misery,
Disgust, the Desire for Deliverance, and Re-observation (a wall of it all at
once, often with an urge to quit practice entirely). It can last days or months.
It is not a sign of doing it wrong. It is the lens turned on the one holding it.

**The work.**

- **Keep noting, gently.** The way out is through: note the fear as *fear*, the
  itch to quit as *wanting to leave*. Shorter sits are fine; stopping entirely
  tends to prolong it.
- **Switch to the heart and the body.** Metta, walking meditation, exercise,
  cold water on the face, time with people.
- **Tummo, the inner fire** (Tibetan; device: *Fire*, 15 min). Rounds of deep
  breathing followed by a held exhale and then a held inhale with a visualised
  flame at the navel. Sit or lie down, on dry land, never in or near water and
  never while driving: breath holds after hyperventilation can cause blackout.
- **Protect sleep.** If sleep drops below six hours night after night, stop
  intensive practice and ease off to 10 to 20 minutes of body-based work.

**The dose.** Whatever keeps you functional. Life first.

**The signs.** The dark stages pass and something cooler and wider opens:
equanimity (Gate VII).

**The shadow.** This one needs saying plainly. Meditation has side effects. In
a review of 83 studies, 8.3% of meditators reported an adverse event, most often
anxiety, depression or unusual perceptions (Farias et al., 2020). Brown
University's Varieties of Contemplative Experience study catalogued more than
fifty kinds of difficult experience in otherwise healthy practitioners (Lindahl
et al., 2017). If you ever feel unreal for days, cannot sleep, have panic you
cannot settle, or intrusive memories or thoughts of self-harm: stop intensive
practice, move your body, sleep, talk to someone, and see a professional.
Cheetah House (Britton's clinic) specialises in exactly this.

**The secret.** Suffering was always there in small print. The dark night
enlarges it until you can read it, and what you can read, you can put down.

**The record.** Mahasi, knowledges five to ten. Ingram, MCTB part IV. Kenneth
Folk's commentaries. Voluntary hyperventilation-and-retention training raised
adrenaline and dampened the innate immune response to endotoxin (Kox et al.,
2014, *PNAS*). Farias et al., 2020, *Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica*. Lindahl
et al., 2017, *PLOS One*.

## Gate VII. Chesed, Jupiter

*Mercy. Equanimity. The wide sky after the storm.*

**The work.**

- **Equanimity.** Sit with everything included and nothing preferred. The
  practice gets easy; the danger is that it gets *boring*. That boredom is the
  point: stay with it, broaden, include the one who is bored.
- **The formless realms** (from the fourth jhana). Expand the stillness to
  *infinite space*; then notice the awareness that knows the space (*infinite
  consciousness*); then that there is nothing there (*nothingness*); then the
  subtlest (*neither perception nor non-perception*).
- **The Microcosmic Orbit** (Taoist neidan; device: *Orbit*, 20 min). Tongue to
  the roof of the mouth. With the inhale, move attention up the spine from the
  perineum to the crown; with the exhale, down the front from the brow to the
  navel and back to the perineum. The orbit links the two main channels.
- **Glimpses** (Loch Kelly, Mahamudra style). Unhook awareness from thought and
  let it drop into the body and open out, knowing from everywhere at once.
- **The headless experiments** (Douglas Harding). Point at a wall, at your
  feet, then at your own face. Look at what is actually at the place you are
  pointing from.

**The dose.** Daily sits of 30 to 60 minutes, and a week of silent retreat a
year if life allows.

**The signs.** Unshakeable calm in the sit; sensations arising and passing
without a flinch; a sense of space around everything; practice that seems to
do itself.

**The shadow.** Mistaking this plateau for the end. Getting complacent.

**The secret.** Nothing needs to change for you to be at peace with it. That is
not resignation. It is the last thing that stands between you and the edge.

**The record.** Mahasi, knowledge eleven (equanimity toward formations).
*Visuddhimagga* on the formless attainments. Mantak Chia, *Awaken Healing
Energy Through the Tao*. Loch Kelly, *The Way of Effortless Mindfulness*.
Douglas Harding, *On Having No Head*.

## Gate VIII. Da'ath, the Abyss

*The hidden sphere. The event horizon. Where he waits.*

**What happens.** The maps agree on something they describe from the outside,
because from the inside there is nothing to describe. In a sit deep in
equanimity, experience speeds and simplifies, and then there is a gap: a brief
cessation of all experience, sometimes called nirodha or the moment of Path and
Fruition. Afterwards: a reset, a clarity, a relief that is hard to overstate.
The Theravada call the first such moment *stream entry*; in Crowley's system it
is the crossing of the Abyss; the Zen word is kensho. Something about the sense
of a self at the centre of experience has permanently changed, and the
traditions say it does not change back.

**The work.**

- Everything from Gate VII, sustained, in a clear and equanimous mind.
- **A teacher.** This is not optional. Find one from a lineage that works with
  these maps (Mahasi-style vipassana, Pragmatic Dharma teachers, or a Zen or
  Tibetan teacher), and check in regularly.
- **A retreat.** Most first crossings happen in multi-day silent retreats, where
  the concentration can stay unbroken.

**The signs.** A discontinuity you can point to only by its edges. Life, and the
mind, run noticeably lighter afterwards.

**The shadow.** Premature claims. Spiritual pride. Chasing a repeat.

**The secret.** He cannot cross to you; he never could. The horizon only ever
opened from this side.

**The record.** Mahasi, knowledges twelve to sixteen (conformity, change of
lineage, path, fruition, review). EEG case studies of voluntary cessation in
an adept show large-scale changes in network organisation before and after the
event (Chowdhury et al., 2023, *Neuropsychologia*; Laukkonen et al., 2023,
*Progress in Brain Research*). Crowley, *One Star in Sight*.

## Gate IX. Binah and Chokmah, Understanding and Wisdom

*The supernals. Emptiness, seen from both sides.*

**The work.**

- **Ways of looking** (Rob Burbea). Take any experience and look at it in a
  different way (as impermanent, as not-self, as dependent on the looking) and
  watch it change. Discover that every self and world is fabricated by how it
  is looked at, and so that none of them is fixed.
- **The further paths.** In the Theravada model, three more cycles through the
  insight stages, each shorter and subtler, leading to once-returning,
  non-returning, and arahantship: the end of greed, aversion and delusion. Other
  lineages count differently. All agree it keeps going.
- **Dream and sleep yoga.** Carry the practice into the night: lucid dreaming
  used for practice, then awareness through dreamless sleep itself.
- **The dark retreat** (Dzogchen tögal). Days or weeks in total darkness,
  only under a qualified teacher. Listed so you know it exists.

**The signs.** Emptiness felt, not theorised. Compassion arising as naturally
as breath.

**The secret.** Emptiness is not a void. It means nothing is stuck, and so
anything can be freed, including the thing you thought you were.

**The record.** Rob Burbea, *Seeing That Frees*. Nagarjuna, *Mulamadhyamakakarika*.
The Theravada four paths as given by Buddhaghosa. Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche on
sleep yoga.

## Gate X. Kether, the Crown

*Thy glory fills all the worlds.*

**The work.**

- **Do nothing** (device: *Do Nothing*, any length). Sit. Let go of all control
  of attention. Whenever you notice yourself controlling, let go of that too.
- **Rigpa.** The Dzogchen recognition of the knowing that was never absent. It
  is pointed out by a teacher, then rested in, then carried into everything.
- **Liber Resh** (Thelema). Four times a day, at dawn, noon, sunset and
  midnight, stop and salute the sun where it is. The device can mark the four
  hours with the bell.

**The signs.** Awareness knows itself. The practice and the day are the same
thing.

**The secret.** There was never a ladder. There was only the horizon, and it
was always as close as the breath.

**The record.** Michael Taft, *Do Nothing*. Shinzen Young. The Dzogchen and
Mahamudra lineages. Aleister Crowley, *Liber Resh vel Helios*.

## The rite of the day

- **Dawn.** Cyclic Sigh, 5 min, before the phone.
- **Morning.** The main sit for your current gate.
- **Through the day.** Self-remembering at every door you walk through. The four
  sun hours, if you choose them.
- **Night.** Twenty-one breaths and the dream intention. The dream book in the morning.
- **Weekly.** One long sit (45 min or more) and one heart sit.
- **Yearly.** One silent retreat, as long as life allows.

## What the Vigil pane keeps

- **Stillness:** total hours of sitting, its own stat, separate from Insight
  (work XP) and from the *meditate* habit.
- **The streak:** days in a row with at least one kept sit.
- **The gate:** your current gate, set by the signs you tick off, not by hours.
- **Per practice:** hours, sits, longest, average.
- **The dream book and the sign log:** dated notes of what happened, to see the
  cycles over months.
