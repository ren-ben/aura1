# The Academy

The university pane: your physics bachelor at the University of Vienna
(curriculum 2026, 180 ECTS). This page says how to read it and how to use it.

## The astrolabe, step by step

Read it like a clock face.

**1. The rings are semesters.** Ring i is the one closest to the eye in the
middle, ring vi the outermost. The small numerals i to vi at 12 o'clock, just
left of the pointer, name them.

**2. Each ring is cut into arcs, and an arc is one module.** A module is a
block of the curriculum you pass as a whole, like *Analysis 1* or
*Experimentalphysik 1*. Its label is its code and its ECTS: `ANA1 8` means
Analysis 1, 8 ECTS.

**3. An arc is as long as its ECTS.** Each ring is one semester's worth of
modules going once round. A module with 8 ECTS out of the semester's 30 fills
8/30 of the circle; a 2 ECTS module is a sliver.

**4. The arcs start at 12 o'clock and go clockwise**, in the order the
curriculum lists the modules. Your first semester, ring i, reads:

| From | Module | ECTS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 o'clock | STEOP1, Experimentalphysik 1 | 8 |
| then | STEOP2, Physikalische Rechenmethoden | 8 |
| then | STEOP3, Forschung und Studium | 2 |
| then | LA1, Lineare Algebra 1 | 4 |
| back to 12 | ANA1, Analysis 1 | 8 |

**5. How an arc is drawn tells you where you stand with it:**

| Looks like | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Solid, bright band | Passed: every course of the module is graded 1 to 4, and together they carry its full ECTS. |
| Band of moving grain | This term: you have added a course of it and it has no grade yet. |
| Thin double outline | Open to you: a module of the semester after yours. |
| Faint dotted trace | Not yet. |

**6. The pointer is the term.** The line from the eye to the rim with a star
at its tip turns once per term: at 12 o'clock on 1 October, back at 12 on
31 January (1 March to 30 June in summer). It says how far the term has run,
not which module you are on. Drag anywhere on the plate to turn it by hand; it
springs back. The tilted ring and the dots riding on it are the rest of the
old instrument, and are only there for the look.

**7. The eye in the middle is the StEOP**, the entry phase every Vienna degree
starts with. You have to pass it (STEOP1 to 3, 18 ECTS) before most of
semester ii opens.

**Hover an arc** to get a card with the module's name, the ECTS you have
earned in it, and each of its courses with its grade. **Click an arc** to mark
it: its books lean out of the shelf and its dates light up in the week. Click
it again to let go.

## The forbidden stacks

Every course you add becomes a grimoire. A module usually has two courses, a
lecture (VO) and its exercise class (UE); each is its own book.

- **Upper shelf:** this term's courses. **Lower shelf:** courses from other
  terms. It only appears once you have some.
- **Order:** left to right in the curriculum's order, so the same order as the
  arcs. A **brass bookend** stands between one module's books and the next.
- **Size:** a book is as thick as its ECTS. A lecture (VO) stands taller than
  an exercise class (UE).
- **Spine:** a rounded, tooled leather spine with raised bands, brass corner
  caps, and sometimes a ribbon hanging out. The module's short name (EP1,
  RM, LA1...) runs up it in blackletter, its module's sigil sits at the head,
  the type (VO, UE) near the foot, and once graded, a **wax seal with the
  grade** at the bottom. A passed book is tooled in gilt; a failed one is
  scored through.
- **Locked:** a StEOP book is strapped shut with an iron band and a padlock
  until you pass it.
- **Around it:** the laws of physics carved in a circle on the back, a
  Newton's cradle on the shelf, and on top a Crookes radiometer turning in
  the lamplight and a black candle.
- **The tag under each group of books** names the module.

**Hover a book** to see its full name, type, ECTS and grade. **Click it** to
take it down. The card that opens lets you:

- **seal its grade:** 1 sehr gut, 2 gut, 3 befriedigend, 4 genügend, 5 nicht
  genügend (a fail). Click the same grade again to take it back.
- **change the module it counts toward:** needed for electives, which the
  server can only guess.
- **pick your exercise group:** UE courses have several groups meeting at
  different times. The first term's are already set to the ones you are
  registered in (EP1 5, Rechenmethoden 10, Lineare Algebra 3, Analysis 7);
  change it here if you swap, and its dates join the week.
- **shelve it** (put it back) or **strike it** (remove it).

## Adding your courses

On an empty shelf, **add the first term** adds all eight first-semester courses
in one go.

To add any other course you need its **u:find number**, the six-digit course
number the university gives every course:

1. Open [ufind.univie.ac.at](https://ufind.univie.ac.at) and search the
   course's name, or browse the course directory (*Vorlesungsverzeichnis*) to
   *Bachelor Physik*.
2. Every course is listed with its number first, for example
   `261608 VO Analysis 1`. That `261608` is the number. The same number is
   shown on the course in u:space.
3. Type it into **add a course by its u:find number** and press **add**.

The course is added to the current term. The server reads its name, type,
ECTS, module, every group's dates and rooms, and any exams u:find has posted,
and guesses the module from the codes u:find lists. A VU (Lineare Algebra)
lists only its exercise dates; its lecture part is read from the note u:find
gives instead (Thursdays 09:15), and shows in the week as a lecture with a map. It asks u:find again
once a day, or when you press **ask again**; the grades, groups and modules you
set are kept.

## Lecture maps

Click a lecture (a VO, a solid block) in **the week**; the arrows in its title
step to the week before or after. A map opens over everything, for that one
lecture, to read the evening before and then write your own notes from.

1. **The letter.** You write to Kael'thos on parchment. The server counts
   which lecture of the term this is and finds the same point in the
   course's syllabus on u:find; that guess is already written in. Beside the
   letter, the syllabus's topics in order, the guessed one underlined: click
   one to write it in, or add what the slides say. Press **seal it** (or
   ctrl + enter): red wax drips onto the ring in the letter's bottom corner,
   and a ring of runes glows round it. Hold the stamp lying beside the
   letter, carry it over the wax and let go: it presses his mark in. The
   letter folds over the seal and flies to him. Nothing is spent until the
   stamp comes down.
2. **The wait,** about a minute, while he reads. A fast model searches the web and takes
   notes (reading the sources), then Kael'thos writes the map from them
   (writing it down). About 11 cents a map, 2 a *deeper*.
3. **The map.** The core, with a small turning model of the topic, sits in
   the middle. Left of it, what to do before you go: the puzzle, the questions
   (click one for its answer; once the lecture has been they read *now,
   without looking*), what to refresh first, and further reading. Right of it,
   what the lecture will contain: the likely order, the words (with their
   German names), the equations, one worked problem, moving plots, and the traps.
4. **Deeper.** Every card has *deeper*, and every line a `+` on hover: new
   cards grow out beside it, and they can go deeper again. Kept, like the map.

Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, **fit** to see it all, Esc to close.
**obsidian** downloads a zip with a `.canvas` laid out as on screen and a
Markdown note with a *my notes* heading; drop both in your vault. **pdf**
prints a plain document version. Opening a map again costs nothing. It stops
for the month once the budget (10 dollars by default) is spent. Exercise
classes have no maps.

## The folios

**folios**, in the week's title (or **its folios** on a book's card), opens
your course material over everything: the Exploded Codex.

1. **Bind Moodle once** in the panel on the right, with your u:account ID
   (the one you type into weblogin, like `musterm99`) and password. The
   server walks weblogin the way the Moodle app does and keeps only Moodle's
   token; your password is not stored. If weblogin refuses it, it is not
   tried again.
2. **Moodle is read** right away and then every morning when you open the
   Academy (or **read it now**): every PDF of your Moodle courses, and the
   flashcard decks with how many cards sit in each box. Files Moodle revises
   are fetched again; nothing else is fetched twice.
3. **The course strip** on top: a lecture and its exercise class are one
   course. **The tomes** under it: each Skript is its own volume; everything
   else in the course is the binder.
4. **The codex** takes the volume apart into its chapters (read from the
   PDF's bookmarks), each slab as thick as its pages. Chapters are named down
   the left; down the right, what belongs to each: the course's other files
   and decks whose names share a word with the chapter. New files fly in.
   Click a chapter to slide it out: the panel shows its first page and what
   hangs off it. Drag to turn it, Esc to let go.
5. **Drop a PDF** anywhere on it (or **add a pdf**) to file it under the
   course on screen. Change what a file is (skript, exercise sheet, slides,
   past exam, other) in the panel; files you added yourself can be struck.

**Ask Kael'thos**, at the foot of the panel: a question about the course on
screen. He finds the pages of its folios that answer it (the lecture's Skript
serves its exercise class too), reads them as printed, equations and all,
and answers with the pages he used; each opens the PDF at that page. The
chapters he cited light up in the codex.

**The ladder**, on an exercise sheet or a past exam: each task (read off
"Aufgabe 1", "Aufgabe 2"...) has four rungs. *Where it stands* points to the
Skript pages and the idea; *a nudge* says how to approach it; *the next step*
writes out only the next step; *show me* is the whole solution. Each opens
only after the one before, so you still solve it yourself.

**Lecture maps** now read your Skript too: its chapters become the topics in
the letter, the pages for the lecture go to Kael'thos as its real source, and
the map lists them under *further*.

If Moodle stops taking the token (you changed your password), the panel says
so and asks you to bind again; the files already read stay.

## The decks

**decks**, in the week's title, opens Kael'thos's table.

- **At the table** he deals one card at a time from the deck chosen top left:
  your Moodle flashcard decks, and your own files. Tap the card to turn it to
  its answer, and again to turn it back, as often as you like. Then judge it
  with the buttons, the arrow keys or a swipe.
  - A Moodle card is *gewusst* or *nicht gewusst*: it climbs Moodle's five
    boxes or falls back to the first, and Moodle is told at once (if it is
    away, the answer waits and goes first at the next sync).
  - One of your own is *again*, *hard*, *good* or *easy*, each showing when
    the card comes back (FSRS, the scheduler Anki uses).
- **Streaks:** answers in a row earn Third Eye (3), Familiar (5), Black Sun
  (7) and Event Horizon (10), each multiplying the insight a card pays.
- **Pictures** with bars are dealt landscape and large.
- **anki**, top right, downloads the deck for Anki's import (File, Import).
- **The desk:** pull the lever. He steps back, and your files are thrown down
  on the table. A file lists its reports; open one to edit it and **refile**,
  **shred** it, or **write a new report**. Kinds: cloze (select words and
  *redact* them; bars with the same number are one card), basic, both ways,
  typed, and image occlusion (attach, drop or paste a picture, drag to lay
  bars, tap a bar to lift it). Maths as `\( \)` everywhere. The proof on the
  right is the card as it will be dealt. Click the file's title to rename it.

## The post

The university inbox (the u:account in `UNIVIE_MAIL_USER`, its password in
`UNIVIE_MAIL_PASSWORD`, both only in the Pi's `.env`) is read over IMAP from
imap.univie.ac.at every five minutes. The newest 400 letters are kept in memory.
Nothing is sent or deleted; the only change made on the server is the read flag,
set when a letter is opened or marked, and cleared again with "mark unread".

It opens as a night sky ("post" on the week bar, or the envelope on the top bar).
- Each sender is a constellation. The eleven busiest get their own and the rest
  share "the scattered".
- A constellation grows a week at a time: each week a sender wrote is one star
  of the figure, stepping off an earlier week's, so a term draws a branching
  figure and new mail only ever adds to it.
- That week's letters gather round its star in a small spiral.
- Unread stars are large and turning; read ones fade with age.

Open a star and it falls, breaks into dust, and the letter comes through the
dither as plain text with its attachments. The device gets the unread count with
every sync and shows an envelope beside the battery while anything is unread.

## The almanac

**month**, in the week's title, opens the whole month over everything: every
lecture, exercise class and exam of your groups, the moon's phase over each
day, and a star on every lecture that already has a map. Click a lecture to
open its map; prev and next (or the arrow keys) turn the months.

## Mnemosyne: everything learned

**mnemosyne** in the week's title bar (with how many topics are fading)
opens one endless map of every topic you have written in. Nothing is made
for you: you write each topic and its key points yourself.

- **Write a topic:** double-click the water, or press **+**. Give it a name
  and its key points, one a line (`$maths$` works). The recall is checked
  against these points.
- **Groups are pools.** Drop a topic into a pool and it joins that group;
  pull it out and it leaves. Choose an area (shift-drag, right-drag, the
  dashed tool, or the Apple Pencil) and press **make a group**. Click a
  group's name to rename it or pick its water; drag the name to move it.
- **A topic's group** is the picker at the top of its panel: any group, no
  group, or a new one. It flows over to the edge of that pool.
- **Its mark:** in the edit panel, pick the broad area it belongs to; the
  mark is drawn still and quiet in place of the plain drop. Physics:
  mechanics, fields, waves, heat, quantum, atoms, matter, relativity.
  Mathematics: analysis, algebra, calculus, chance, proof. Craft: lab, code.
- **Links:** drag from a topic to one that builds on it with the link tool
  (or alt-drag).
- **Hubs:** switch **a hub** on in a topic's edit panel and it stops being
  something to recall: it holds every topic linked to it, drawn as a
  membrane round them with a tendril to each. Link a hub to another hub and
  the one you dragged from holds the other (its panel can **turn it
  around**). Dragging a hub carries everything in it; its panel recalls just
  its fading topics.
- **The water is the schedule.** A topic you hold well sits deep in its pool.
  As you forget it, the pool draws back from it until it is a lone drop, and
  once it is due it ripples. A small bead is a topic not learned yet: **mark
  learned** starts its clock.
- **The recall:** **recall what is fading** takes everything due, most
  faded first. Write the topic out on paper or in Notability (an hourglass
  keeps five minutes), press **done writing** (space), tick the key points
  that are on your page, and rate it (1 to 4). The rating your ticks suggest
  is lit. FSRS sets when it comes back, in days.

## The rest of the pane

- **The tally:** ECTS passed out of 180, this term's ECTS, the StEOP out of 18,
  your ECTS-weighted grade over passed courses, and the next exam.
- **Exams ahead:** the next exams u:find lists for your courses or their StEOP
  module, with the days left. Hover one to mark its module.
- **The week:** this week's dates for your courses (lectures, and the
  exercise groups you picked), with a line at the current hour. A lecture
  opens its map; an exercise class marks its module.
- **The rector's armillary:** six beads on its tilted ring, one per semester.
  A bead lights as its semester's modules are passed; the current one pulses.

## The lore

Vienna's university was founded in 1365, and its first chairs were paid to
watch the sky. The registrar here keeps the old habits: every course is a book
on a shelf, every grade a wax seal, and the degree is read off an astrolabe,
the way Miskatonic kept its restricted stacks. Kael'thos approves. He says the
mathematics is the telescope, and that the eye at the pole is there because
the first thing a degree teaches you is how to look.
